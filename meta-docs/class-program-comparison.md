# Class program: FY15–19 (pre-Covid) vs FY23–26 (post-Covid)

*Attendees = ticket records with SUCCEEDED status; gross = registration dollars as
recorded in Neon (includes refunded canceled sections; walk-up tickets booked to
internal accounts stay in). Period A = FY2015–2019 (5 years), Period B = FY2023–2026
(4 years), both averaged per year. FY20–22 (Covid transition) excluded. Series and
family grouping follow `scripts/attendance_doc.py`. Local analysis 2026-08-31 (economics
section added 2026-09-01); not committed. Neon itself carries no pay data — instructor
economics come from the books (last section).*

## Headline

| Per year | A: FY15–19 | B: FY23–26 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Class sections that ran | 118 | 82 | **−30%** |
| Attendees | 1,904 | 1,684 | −12% |
| Gross registration $ | $102,193 | $91,251 | −11% |
| Attendees per section (mean / median) | 16.1 / 8 | 20.4 / 9 | bigger, not smaller |
| Gross $ per attendee | $54 | $54 | flat |
| Youth share of attendees | 34% | 47% | +13 pts |

**The decline is volume of offerings, not class size or price.** A third fewer sections
run; the ones that run are slightly larger (median 8 → 9; the mean rises because
100+‑attendee youth-theatre events are a bigger share of the mix); price per attendee is
unchanged. What changed is *what* is offered.

## What replaced what

| Attendees per year | A | B | A→B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Youth theatre combined** (CCS musicals + HH camps + showcases) | 603 | 1,050 | **+74%** |
| **Everything else** (adult & general enrichment) | 1,301 | 634 | **−51%** |

The youth-theatre complex grew from $11.4k to $30.0k gross per year while everything
else fell from $90.8k to $61.3k. Within "everything else," the decline is broad:

| Family (attendees/yr) | A | B | |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Yoga, wellness & fitness | 230 | 182 | −21% |
| Irish dance | 120 | 74 | −38% |
| Visual arts | 113 | 60 | −47% |
| Bollywood dance & fencing | 44 | 16 | −64% |
| Music instruction | 39 | 9 | −77% |
| Misc / other (Tai Ji & Qi Gong, dance showcases, chorale, one-off workshops) | 587 | 88 | **−85%** |
| Dance (ballet/tap/jazz) | 151 | 156 | flat |
| Hip hop | 18 | 50 | +178% |

Period A's catalog carried adult enrichment that is mostly gone: Tai Ji & Qi Gong
(~79 attendees/yr across sections), the BollyX fitness wave (21-attendee sections in
FY18), one-off workshops, youth chorale, music instruction. Period B's catalog is
youth-theatre-heavy plus a steady dance core.

## The recent slide — FY25–26 vs FY23–24

| Per year | FY23–24 | FY25–26 | |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Attendees | 2,210 | 1,158 | −48% |
| Gross $ | $85,304 | $97,197 | **+14%** |
| $ per attendee | $39 | $84 | ×2.2 |
| CCS Drama Club musicals (Acting & improv family) | 1,100 | ~0 | gone |

The CCS partnership musicals ran through FY24 and then stopped; they were half the
post-Covid attendance at ~$10 a head. Their absence is the entire FY25–26 attendance
drop — every other family held or grew, and revenue *rose* because what remains (camps
at $222/attendee, dance at $134–160, Irish at $160) prices far higher than the CCS
tickets. If "much higher earlier" means the last two years vs pre-Covid: FY25–26
averages 1,158 attendees/yr vs A's 1,904 (−39%) while gross is only −5% ($97.2k vs
$102.2k).

**Reconciling the two revenue columns.** The books' "class & workshop income" tracks our
*traditional classes* revenue (FY19 differs by only $660; FY15–19 within $1.8–19k). The
post-Covid gap (Neon $11–22k higher, FY23–26) is the **youth-theatre complex** — camps,
showcases, and the CCS musicals ($24–36k/yr now) — which our categorization counts as
classes but the books book to other program lines. Both bases are right on their own
question; class-economics comparisons should use the books line or split the complex out.

**The combined story.** Fee regimes shifted twice: a sliding scale from 50/50
(6-student minimum) through FY22; straight 70/30 in FY23–24; stated 63% of income net of
a 10% admin fee from FY25. The instructor share went from a 43.9% average (FY02–19) to
~65% (FY23–26), and the FY27 budget plans 73.6%. Meanwhile the price gains found on the
Neon side ($36–41 per attendee in FY23–24 rising to $78–91) flowed to instructors under
the new regimes: net to Hubbard Hall fell from $50–62k/yr pre-Covid to $24–32k FY23–26,
on gross (either basis) that held up. Scholarships grew from $1.9k (FY24) to $8.2k
(FY25) to $19.8k (FY26 preliminary) and $20k budgeted. Their sheet's own caveats apply:
FY23–24 income lines do not tie to the board worksheet, FY19–20 statements were not in
the board Drive, and FY20 shows a $4,501 history-vs-statement discrepancy.
